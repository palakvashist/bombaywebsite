 The interior of Bombay’s cotton textile mills confronts the limits of factory protection more starkly than legislative debate alone ever could. From the 1880s onward, factory inspectors repeatedly returned to the same set of observations: closed windows, saturated air,[^1] excessive heat, and bodies laboring within atmospheres engineered for cotton rather than for people. These descriptions recur with such regularity across inspection reports, correspondence, and committee proceedings that they cannot be read as incidental commentary. They constitute a parallel archive of industrial environment, one that developed alongside factory law yet remained largely detached from its regulatory force. The law restricted migration.[^2]
 
[^1]: Kumar Arun, *Silent Rebellion* (Cambridge, 2026), 15.

[^2]: Alexandra Emma, *Labor Matters* (harvard University Press, 2024), 13.
